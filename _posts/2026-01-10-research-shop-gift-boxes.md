I spend most of my time building and operating precision experiments—where small mechanical details, repeatability, and careful documentation can make or break performance. It turns out those habits translate surprisingly well to non-research projects.

For my wedding party, I decided to make a set of gift boxes using the research shop. The goal was simple: something personal, cleanly made, and durable enough to feel like a “real object,” not a last-minute craft.

## The engineering mindset carries over

Even for a small project, the workflow felt familiar:

- **Requirements:** size constraints, what needs to fit, and what should be visible when opened  
- **Materials:** balancing appearance, machinability, and finish  
- **Tolerances:** making parts that actually mate cleanly (especially lids and inserts)  
- **Iteration:** prototype one, learn what matters, then build the set  
- **Documentation:** so every box comes out consistent

## What I built

The final design used a simple geometry with a focus on clean edges and repeatable assembly. The most time-consuming part wasn’t cutting—it was refining the process so the “Nth copy” looked as good as the first.

## Why I like projects like this

These builds are a fun reminder that experimental physics is as much about *craft* as it is about equations: translating a concept into a reliable object, learning what fails, and improving the design until it’s robust.
